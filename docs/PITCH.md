# Peer-review pitch notes

## 35-second segment for the combined solo pitch

"Common Ground is my City of Espoo entry. It starts with a concrete company task and a transferable work sample, then proposes a small introduction schedule. Here, a café worker's waste tracker is a reason to talk to a food-systems team. Both parties agree before a table-and-time card exists. If someone cancels, a replacement needs fresh agreement. The code and frozen synthetic audit are public. It solves a booking and explanation workflow; it does not claim to measure hiring quality."

Demo one pair and a card. Keep this project at the same table as the other solo entries. The bridge records the organiser's confirmation of a combined peer-review table. Do not claim a separate table or appointment.

## Five-minute standalone notes

0:00–0:40: Espoo's official brief describes vague company needs, transferable skills hidden by CVs and too many irrelevant approaches. Show the organiser's first screen. All people and employers are fictional.

0:40–1:40: Open Talent 01 + Loop Kitchen. Read the concrete kitchen task and the café tracker sample. Explain that the essential tags and mutual choices are explicit synthetic fixture facts, not model-inferred competence. Show what the company can teach. Contrast a task and a sample with a CV score.

1:40–2:25: Record the talent's answer, show that the card remains unavailable, then record the company's answer. Create a card. Table letter, time and conversation starter support an actual in-person introduction. Demo agreement is local; no real invitation is sent.

2:25–3:00: Cancel the pair and rebuild. Explain that existing mutually agreed meetings are locked. A replacement has no inherited consent. Open Company needs to show Harbour Works' missing task, or Work evidence to mark someone absent.

3:00–4:05: Audit tab. 12 development variants across three families, 16 held-out variants across four whole families. Evidence schedules valid 16/16 held-out versus 8/16 for a deliberately simple keyword-CV baseline. Independent enumeration confirms maximum cardinality. 168 nuisance and 12 cancellation checks pass. These measure synthetic rule conformance, not human hiring relevance or a deployed-scale advantage.

4:05–4:40: Show the failure. Scarcity leaves some eligible people without slots. The synthetic career-change group gets 40/42 slots and the direct-path group 27/38. Group labels are ignored, yet allocation differs. Real deployment needs a reviewed access policy, human evidence review, secure shared confirmation, consent/privacy controls and legal assessment.

4:40–5:00: Invite the judge to cancel a meeting themselves. End on a practical, human-approved introduction, not an accuracy score.

## Answers to likely questions

Why no LLM? The actual job here is making a useful introduction and a valid schedule. Structured task/evidence entry avoids pretending that polished CV prose is verified capability. Assisted evidence extraction could be explored later with human review and separate evaluation.

Does it use stable matching? No. It maximises meeting count, then explicit preference points. It can leave blocking pairs and unequal access. We do not claim stability.

Does this comply with GDPR or the EU AI Act? The prototype uses synthetic data and no applicant intake. It has not received independent legal review. A real pilot would require that review and consent, retention, deletion and human governance controls.

What would you test next? Whether employers and participants agree with the task/sample mapping, whether the explanation helps a real conversation, and how to distribute scarce slots fairly. Use independently authored scenarios and consented pilot evidence.
